# Apex 2.2.0

Everything since 2.1.1 in one release. The portals were rebuilt for real screens and real
languages, the desk was reorganised around pages an operator opens, and a long list of
refusals, silent failures and wrong defaults were corrected.

## The portals

- **They fit the screen you are on.** The worker, driver, housing, safety and fleet portals stayed a phone-width column on a tablet or a monitor; they now put the list beside the open item, and keep the action you need next within thumb reach on a phone.
- **One design across all of them.** Dark mode no longer leaves panels white, four buttons that rendered nothing show their icons, two portals that kept their own copy of the colours now read the shared set, and the keyboard outline is back.
- **They read in your language.** The trip-progress, trip-rating and boarding screens were English for workers reading Urdu, Hindi or Bengali. The Masar and driver link screens, the copy and WhatsApp actions and the expiry warning are now Arabic too, and a refusal raised on an Arabic screen arrives in Arabic.
- **They load without the public internet.** The map code and every portal typeface are served by the app itself. The map background still comes from OpenStreetMap unless you point `apex_map_tile_url` at your own tile source.
- **Home screens that answer one question.** The worker and driver home screens show the ride, what needs attention, the bed and the contacts as separate cards, with the next action pinned within reach.
- **The ride screen says where the bus is** — scheduled, on the way with its arrival time, at your stop, departed, or finished.
- Filling in a transport request and pausing no longer clears what you typed, and a button pressed during a background refresh is no longer ignored.
- A downward drag anywhere on a worker screen no longer stops the page scrolling.
- The Arabic font is loaded rather than substituted, and a bad driver link shows a message instead of a raw error string.

## The route supervisor board

- **Every driver on one map**, with filters by driver and by plan, instead of one driver at a time.
- **The route is drawn along the streets it uses**, with stops numbered, so a bus off route is visible at a glance.
- The map draws as soon as you open the tab instead of a blank box, and a wide screen keeps it beside the list and the plan.
- **Approvals have their own screen**, each plan with approve and reject on the row.
- **A link names the plan and the section**, so it opens exactly that screen and the back button walks back through it.
- The board now uses the same side menu, header and colours as the other supervisor screens, and the trip status reads in the language of the page.
- Tab and Escape stay inside a dialog instead of walking onto controls behind it.

## The desk

- **Nine workspaces became five.** Salis is one page again with a Masar card; Habitat's four became Housing and Safety, and Custody and Costs.
- **Fourteen record types were reachable from no page at all**, including every telecom record. All now have a home, and Logistay gained the workspace its module never had.
- Twelve links and two whole cards had disappeared from Backend Engines, and two more from Compliance and Rentals. They are back.
- **Every list row carries a coloured status**, so a list can be read without opening it.
- The Apex Core page listed 31 machinery tables an operator never opens; it now holds the four settings.
- A housing supervisor no longer sees ERPNext Settings, Integrations, Build and Users.
- **Pickers show names, not ids** — a plate, a person or a number instead of VEH-000042 — and offer only what belongs to the record you are on.
- 24 workflow buttons and 7 workflow states rendered in English and grey on an Arabic desk.
- The service order, dispatch trip, route plan and rental settlement forms carry the action you take next, shown only where it applies.
- Ninety labels moved to plain wording, and several that said the wrong thing were corrected — one told a driver to take a vehicle out where the screen releases it.

## Setup and demo

- **Every page of the setup wizard reads in Arabic**, including each checkbox and the sentence explaining what leaving it off means.
- **A step for reviewing every alert the site sends**, each with its own switch and warning time.
- **The last page offers demo data** — one accommodation site with rooms, beds, a resident and an open maintenance request. Off by default, and removable afterwards from Apex Settings, which cancels a submitted record before deleting it.
- Running the demo seeder on a fresh site now says why it did nothing: there is no company until the wizard creates one.
- Six go-live checklists, one per page you can open, including a Logistay checklist that did not exist.
- Forty-one checklist steps carried guidance no screen ever showed; it now appears where the step is described.

## Money, custody and payroll

- Cancelling a damage assessment left the payroll deduction it raised queued against the employee; cancelling a safety task left its repair tickets open in front of the technicians. Both are now withdrawn.
- **A rejected cost recovery** was left with no action and no cancel button, so it could never be closed.
- **Accounting can record the intercompany sign-off after submit**, and the record names who gave it. It cannot be given by the person who submitted the movement.
- A damage recovery whose deduction posts to a different account than the advance was paid from is now refused as you save it, with both accounts named — never during a payroll run.
- A handover could only be approved when the sending side had ticked the verification box before submitting, so the receiving side could never confirm the check they had made.
- An amended utility bill no longer carries the payment link of the document it amends.
- A purchase request can now be raised for a telecom billing period that has already ended, which is the normal case.

## Movement and fleet

- **A worker could confirm he was on a 07:00 bus at 23:00**, and a bus that had already passed his stop still accepted him, so the gate manifest counted a man standing at the camp.
- A driver could save another driver's trip start record: the check meant to stop it had been inert since drivers moved to barcode links.
- **A new vehicle assignment proposed the outgoing driver**, so submitting without noticing reassigned the vehicle to the person it was being taken from.
- A stolen vehicle read as an ordinary stop on the fleet board, so its stripe, its count and its recover button never appeared.
- Sorting the fleet board by any column left the order unchanged, and its Area filter matched nothing.
- A vehicle whose category name merely contained a word like motorbike was filed under motorcycles.
- The driver's boarding manifest did not repaint when a worker confirmed boarding.
- A transport request could never be authorized by whoever raised it, but the form did not say so; the rule is now on the field.
- Fleet supervisors, managers, project managers and the finance manager can select a Project on a transport request; the picker used to refuse them outright.
- A bulk vehicle action now refuses more than 50 vehicles at once, and a stock count more than 200 lines, instead of holding the screen.
- Every SIM custody action in Telecom Control left the desk frozen until the page was reloaded.

## Housing and safety

- **A Housing Assignment could not be created from the form at all**: picking a building, room or bed cleared the value you had just picked.
- The Arrivals Desk opened to a permission dialog for the very people it is meant for, and the Active Resident Register hung on Loading as soon as there was one resident.
- **Safety verdicts marked during a round looked saved but were lost on reload.** They are kept per building and day until you submit, and the screen says they are a draft.
- Submitting a round that needs a photo was refused by the server with nothing on screen; the refusal now names the task and the missing photo.
- The reminder to attach a completion photo arrived only once the work order already had one.
- **A lease could not be opened and saved again once its end date had passed.**
- A room with no bed capacity set could show one occupancy status and be given another by the weekly sync.
- Twenty-one seeded safety tasks quoted a named client's audit finding in the instructions an operator reads; each now states the requirement without naming anyone.
- The bed-occupancy indexes now reach sites installed before the fix.

## Alerts, notifications and access

- **Alerts still reached users who had switched notifications off** in their own settings, and so did the daily and weekly digests.
- A safety reminder that linked a building its audience cannot read now names the building in the message instead.
- A permission granted for one kind of record no longer widens what its holder sees in the others.
- A user holding only the SIM Operations role sees their surface in the sidebar again, and the Government Relations Officer and Internal Auditor reach the Salis branch instead of a blank sidebar.
- The finance approver can open the custody damage register, not only the records it emails them.
- Two housing reports now ask the one place that decides which buildings a supervisor may see.
- A photo that is not really an image is refused when attaching a shift or request photo; before, a renamed text file was stored as a picture.
- A nightly cleanup reclaims oversized access-log rows that age-based retention cannot see.

## Under the hood

Nothing below changes what you see. Scheduled jobs no longer lose a whole run to one bad row; a migration that fails every row now refuses to be recorded as done rather than reporting success over data that never moved; and the worker transport screen asks the server once for all rides instead of once each.
