# Apex 2.8.0

Apex now has its own tile on the app screen, and the alerts that warn you before something lapses reach the people who act on them.

- **Apex opens from the app screen.** The screen lists a tile for the Apex desk alongside the portals, and every tile carries its own name, so the worker portal and the driver portal are no longer two entries reading the same.
- **A supervisor reads the inspections taken on his building.** The building supervisor, the maintenance technician and the accommodation manager can open a maintenance inspection report; a supervisor sees the buildings he holds and no others.
- **An expiring telecom contract warns you like an expiring licence.** It joins the vehicle compliance and building licence alerts, and a rent payment that falls due now warns a week ahead instead of on the day.
- **Switching email off switches it off.** Turning the email setting off stops every alert this app would send, and the setting says exactly that on the screen, including that other apps on the site keep their own email.
- **Money reads in your own currency.** Cards, charts and alerts take the currency from the site instead of showing a fixed one.
- **A new site opens its portals.** A freshly installed site reaches the worker and driver portals and the rest without a manual step first.
- **A bed changes in front of you.** The housing screens show a bed being taken the moment it is taken, so two people at the front desk stop discovering the clash when the second one saves.
- **A double click cannot post twice.** Custody stock, an asset move, a completed trip and a driver's attendance each accept one posting per record, so a repeated click or a retried save no longer doubles a figure nobody would catch until a stock count.
- **Turning approvals off turns them off.** The switch now stops the fleet approval steps it names, and it stays off after an update instead of switching itself back on.
- **What an administrator decides, stays decided.** Disabling a role, and the settings an administrator changes, survive the next update instead of returning to how the app shipped.
- **The setup wizard asks seven questions instead of nineteen.** Everything it stopped asking has its own screen and a safe default, and one of the questions it asked could fail the installation outright.
- **A report queued to your department reaches you.** An idle resident report assigned to human resources opens for the manager it was assigned to, where before it reached nobody.
- **A lease that starts on the thirty-first stays on the thirty-first.** Its later due dates no longer slide to the twenty-eighth after the first short month.
- **A part-done batch says so.** Triaging many requests at once reports what actually saved instead of reporting success after putting everything back.
